# GTM Strategy & Success Dashboard (Module 6)

## Get your prior work ready
- **The feature you're launching from your M4 PRD, one feature, not a list:** Pre-Submission Answer Check (my M4 Now feature, tested in M5): before a security questionnaire is sent, it checks every answer against current certifications, regions and policies, flags the ones a change has made outdated, and lets the sales engineer fix them or send only those to security.
- **What your M5 experiment told you shipped / iterating, the evidence behind the launch:** The experiment hasn't run, so there's no real result yet. This plan is gated on it: Phase 2 only goes ahead if the week-12 read meets my M5 ship rule (questionnaires with no "to confirm" answers up at least 17 points, 58% to 75% or better, at p < 0.05, turnaround no more than 0.5 day slower than control, and at least 240 questionnaires from 8 hypothetical design-partner companies). If the read says Iterate, the readouts still happen but there's no contract offer, and the check goes back for one more round. If it says Investigate (a guardrail broke, or companies disagree), nothing launches until I know why.
- **Your persona pull your M2 persona, this anchors your audience:** A sales engineer at a mid-market SaaS company who answers buyer security questionnaires against deal deadlines. They can't tell which answers a certification, region or policy change has made outdated, and security takes 3 to 4 days to reply, so answers go out marked "to confirm".

## Set your goal, then your audience
- **Primary GTM goal awareness · engagement · conversion, pick one:** Conversion
- **Why this goal? what makes this the right goal for this feature right now:** The people who matter first already know the check: 8 design-partner companies will have used it on half their questionnaires for 12 weeks. Awareness would be the wrong goal, because nobody outside a small set of mid-market SaaS companies needs to hear about it yet, and the experiment already measures whether sales engineers use it. What the bet hasn't proven is that a company will pay for it, so the launch has to turn pilot results into signed contracts.
- **Target audience the specific segment your goal implies, be precise:** The budget owner and the approver at the 8 hypothetical design-partner companies: the head of sales engineering (signs) and the security lead (has to trust the check). All are mid-market SaaS companies sending about 13 security questionnaires a month with no proposal team. Second, a named list of 30 lookalike companies with the same profile (hypothetical). The users stay my M2 persona, the sales engineers who run the check.

## Size your launch
- **Launch tier S (minimal) · M (targeted) · L (multi-channel) · XL (full GTM):** M (targeted)
- **Justification reach + revenue impact + what silence would risk:** Reach is small: 8 companies sending about 100 questionnaires a month, plus 30 named prospects. Revenue impact is the bet's first paid contracts, which decides whether it gets funded past the pilot, but it isn't a market moment. Silence would let the design partners treat it as a free pilot that quietly ends at week 12, with no proof anyone will pay. Not L: there's no market to make aware, and paid media can't reach 38 named companies better than a direct conversation.
- **Is this a launch or a release? does it need go-to-market, or can it just ship?:** Both, in order. For the 8 design partners it's a release: switch the check on for every questionnaire, no campaign. For the 30 lookalike companies it's a launch, because nothing reaches them unless sales takes the design-partner results to them.

## Choose your top three channels and plan assets
- **Channel 1 owned / earned / paid, and why it reaches your audience:** Owned: Pilot results readout with each design partner in week 13: a 30-minute call with their head of sales engineering and security lead, showing their own clean-questionnaire rate, turnaround and the answers the check caught, then the contract offer. They already meet us every two weeks during the pilot, so this is where they are.
- **Channel 2 owned / earned / paid, and why:** Earned: Design-partner references: 2 case studies and reference calls from security leads who agree to them. Mid-market buyers check with peers before buying anything that touches security answers, and a security lead saying "I trust what my SEs send now" is more credible than anything we say.
- **Channel 3 owned / earned / paid, and why:** Owned: Direct outreach from our account executive to the head of sales engineering at each of the 30 named lookalike companies, warm where possible: each design partner that signs is asked for 2 introductions to peers. The email leads with a case study and an offer to run the check on one questionnaire they've already sent. These companies aren't in our ecosystem yet, so this is the only channel that reaches them. No paid channel: an M launch doesn't justify media spend, and an ad can't target 30 named companies better than one email.
- **Enablement & assets what Sales / CS / Support need, plus the assets to build (one-pager, demo, etc.):** An enablement brief for the account executive and customer success: what it does (checks drafted answers against current certifications, regions and policies before submission and sends only flagged answers to security), what it doesn't do (draft answers, or replace security review), what changes for SEs (one check before submitting) and for security (fewer, more specific questions), and the 3 objections we expect, with answers: "our AI answer tool already keeps the library fresh" (it's fresh when someone uploads a new document; the check is triggered by the change), "one more step for SEs" (M5 turnaround guardrail), and "who keeps the posture register current" (a named owner at the customer, set up by CS). Assets: a one-page pilot results summary, a 3-minute demo, the 2 case studies, a register setup checklist and design-partner pricing.

## Make it executable
- **Ownership named owner per key activity, individual, not department:** Me (PM): the go/no-go at the week-12 read, each design partner's results summary, design-partner pricing (with finance, set by week 10), the launch tracker and the metrics. Account executive (one person): readouts, contracts and the 30-company outreach. Product marketing manager (one person): enablement brief, one-pager and case studies, including asking design partners for case-study permission during Phase 1. Customer success manager (one person): register setup at each new customer, the 30-day check-in, and making sure each customer names one person who keeps their posture register current. Pilot engineer (one person): switches the check on for every questionnaire and owns the feature flag. The team is hypothetical, so these are roles; each gets a name before sign-off.
- **Budget & resource gaps what costs extra, and any asset you can't currently build:** Costs extra, to request before week 10: case-study writing and design (about 2 weeks of a freelancer), a year-one design-partner discount (finance sign-off before the readouts), and about a week of engineering for a demo on realistic data, because the prototype (answer-check-prototype.lovable.app) uses a hard-coded sample questionnaire. No media budget. Gap: no one on our side checks that a customer's posture register stays current after setup. The plan asks each customer to name an owner, and the customer success manager checks the register's last-changed dates at the 30-day check-in, but there's no ongoing check yet, so the check can go stale the same way the answers did.
- **Timeline Phase 1 beta → Phase 2 launch moment → Phase 3 post-launch:** Phase 1, beta (weeks 1 to 12): the M5 A/B test with 8 design partners doubles as the beta. Collect objections, quotes and case-study permission; enablement brief, one-pager and demo ready by week 10. Read date: end of week 12. Phase 2, launch moment (weeks 13 to 14, only if the read says Ship): readouts and contract offers to all 8, the check switched on for every questionnaire at partners who sign, case studies published, outreach to the 30 companies starts. Phase 3, post-launch (weeks 15 to 26): conversions and usage reviewed weekly, 30-day check-ins, post-launch decision at the end of week 26.

## Define how you'll know it worked
- **Success metrics 2 to 3 metrics that match your GTM goal:** 1) Design-partner conversion: paid contracts signed within 30 days of the readout, target 6 of 8. 2) Lookalike pipeline: of the 30 named companies, 6 run the check on a past questionnaire by week 20 and 2 sign by week 26. 3) Retention signal at paying customers: the check runs on at least 90% of questionnaires, and questionnaires with no "to confirm" answers stay at or above 75% (my M5 ship floor) for 8 weeks after launch. All targets are hypothetical.
- **Bad signal to watch for e.g. high reach, zero signups = message-market mismatch:** Design partners keep using it but won't sign: the check runs on most questionnaires, yet fewer than 4 of 8 have signed 30 days after the readout. That's trials with no upgrades, a value-proposition problem: the SE feels the value but the budget owner doesn't. The fix is the pitch (lead with security review time saved and deals that didn't slip), not a longer pilot. Early warning at a paying customer: the check runs on fewer than 70% of questionnaires by week 4 after launch, which means SEs are skipping it and the renewal is at risk; the customer success manager calls that customer's head of sales engineering that week. For the 30 lookalike companies: fewer than 3 replies by week 18 means the message isn't landing with people who haven't seen the pilot; I change the email's lead, not the list.
- **Most likely post-launch decision double-down · iterate · pivot · deprioritize, and what would trigger it:** Most likely Iterate: change one variable, the pitch to the budget owner, because 8 design partners is a small base and first contracts usually stall on price or on who owns the posture register. Double down if 6 of 8 sign and the 75% clean rate holds for 8 weeks: take it to the next 100 lookalike companies as an L launch. Deprioritize if fewer than 3 sign and the clean rate falls back toward the 58% baseline.
