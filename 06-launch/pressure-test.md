# Pressure test: GTM launch plan (Module 6)

> I ran the GTM Launch Plan Builder's pressure-test prompt on my first draft, acting as a senior product marketing leader, and fixed what it found before exporting the final plan. All numbers are hypothetical (assumptions, not measurements).

## First draft I tested

- **Goal:** Conversion, turn the 8 design-partner pilots into paid contracts.
- **Audience:** Head of sales engineering (signs) and security lead (approves) at the 8 hypothetical design partners, then 30 named lookalike mid-market SaaS companies.
- **Tier:** M, Targeted.
- **Channels:** 1) Owned: results readout with each design partner. 2) Earned: 2 case studies and reference calls. 3) Owned: account executive emails the 30 lookalike companies with a case study. No paid.
- **Ownership:** Me (PM) for the go/no-go, tracker and metrics; account executive, product marketing manager, customer success manager and pilot engineer as one person each.
- **Metrics:** 6 of 8 design partners sign within 30 days; 6 of 30 lookalikes run the check and 2 sign by week 26; check runs on 90% of questionnaires and the clean rate stays at 75% or more.
- **Bad signal:** check used on most questionnaires, but fewer than 4 of 8 sign.

## Review

| # | Question | Finding | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does the audience follow from the goal, or did I default to awareness? | It follows. The users already know the check from the pilot, and engagement is what the M5 test measures. The open question is whether anyone pays, so conversion is right and the audience is the people who sign, not the SEs. One gap: the plan only covered a Ship result. | Added what happens on Iterate (readouts, no contract offer) and Investigate (nothing launches until I know why). |
| 2 | Is the tier honest? | Yes. 8 companies and 30 prospects is targeted, and revenue is the bet's first contracts, not a market moment. L would mean paying for reach the audience doesn't need. | No change. Kept "no paid" with the reason written down. |
| 3 | Does each channel reach this audience where they already are? | Channels 1 and 2 do: design partners already meet us every two weeks, and buyers check with peers. Channel 3 doesn't: it's labelled Owned, but the 30 lookalike companies aren't in our ecosystem, and a cold email to a head of sales engineering is easy to ignore. | Channel 3 is now warm where possible: each design partner that signs is asked for 2 introductions to peers. Wrote down that it's the only channel that reaches non-customers. |
| 4 | Named owner for every activity, every budget or asset gap surfaced? | Mostly. Owners are roles because the team is hypothetical. Two activities had no owner: setting the design-partner price and writing each partner's results summary. Budget asks were there, with a deadline. | I own both, pricing with finance by week 10. The posture-register gap stays listed as a gap, with the customer success manager checking it at day 30. |
| 5 | Do the metrics match the goal, and is the bad signal a real early warning? | The metrics are conversion and retention, which match. The design-partner bad signal is actionable. There was no early warning for the 30 lookalikes, and "2 sign by week 26" had no midpoint to check against. | Added a week-20 checkpoint (6 run the check) and an early warning: fewer than 3 replies by week 18 means change the email's lead, not the list. |

## Not changed

- **Owners as roles, not names.** The team is hypothetical. In a real company each line gets a person's name before sign-off.
- **Targets.** 6 of 8 and 2 of 30 are my assumptions, not benchmarks. They're written down so a stakeholder can hold me to them, and the week-26 decision says what each result means.
