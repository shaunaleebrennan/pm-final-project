Problem Hook & Value Proposition

Responses

  • Chosen path, which scenario are you committing to? (StreamLine or RouteLogic, or your own): My own initiative (B2B): a pre-submission check for security questionnaire answers. Security questionnaires sit in almost every enterprise SaaS deal, and AI has made answers faster to write but no easier to keep accurate. I picked this because it's a high-stakes B2B workflow I want to understand deeply.
  • Strategic crisis, based on the brief, if the company does nothing for 12 months, what happens to its market position?: If we do nothing for 12 months, the company will keep submitting security answers that were true last year: an expired certification scope, a data residency option still in beta, a policy that has since changed. Those answers become part of the contract. Enterprise buyers who find a mismatch in due diligence or audit slow the deal or walk away, a wrong answer that gets signed becomes legal exposure, and the security team spends more of its week reviewing sales paperwork than securing the product. Questionnaire turnaround becomes the slowest step in late-stage deals.
  • Moment of misery, what is the user likely doing outside the product (Google, spreadsheets, a competitor) because your tool isn’t serving them?: The user is currently forced to paste answers from a questionnaire done eight months ago and let an AI assistant fill the gaps on a 250-question questionnaire due Friday. For a dozen answers they aren't sure (is EU data residency live yet? does the SOC 2 report cover the new product?), so they post in the security team's Slack channel and wait days, because security is answering the same questions for five other deals. Under deadline, some answers go out unchecked.
  • Problem hook, summarize the business risk and user pain into one urgent sentence.: We must solve the risk of signing enterprise deals on security answers that are no longer true by addressing sales engineers' reliance on copied, AI-finished answers and a security team that can't keep up with review.
  • Value proposition, based on the proposed initiative (Spotlight or Velocity), what is the new value and why is it urgent to launch now?: For sales engineers and proposal teams answering enterprise security questionnaires, and the security and GRC teams who review them, we will check every drafted answer against current certifications, data residency and policies before submission, flag any answer that a certification, region or policy change has made outdated or overstated, and send only those answers to security for review because enterprise buyers are asking for more security evidence in every deal, AI has made answers fast but not correct, and every inaccurate answer that gets signed is a contractual risk.

Cold-read your own hook

  • Is the business risk high enough to justify a new initiative, a high-stakes threat, or a minor inconvenience?: Yes. Answers become contract terms, delays hit late-stage revenue, and a wrong answer is legal and reputational exposure.
  • Is the moment of misery a systemic problem or just an edge case?: Systemic. Nearly every enterprise deal includes a questionnaire, and the posture changes whenever a certification, region or policy changes.
  • Does the value proposition actually remove the obstacle you identified, or is it a generic feature?: It targets the gap AI answer tools leave. They draft answers from whatever documents have been uploaded, and their freshness depends on someone uploading current ones. Nothing flags when a certification, region or policy change has made an answer the team already relies on untrue. This check is triggered by the change, not by the next upload. Module 2 needs to prove that against AI answer tools (Conveyor, Loopio, Responsive) and trust centres.

  • Your journey map, a shareable link, or the map file you committed (e.g. journey-map.html): [02-discovery/journey-map.md](https://github.com/shaunaleebrennan/pm-final-project/blob/main/02-discovery/journey-map.md)

Future-state journey map

1. Prepare · opens questionnaire, sees matched answers with owners → starts from answers tied to live sources · copied answers carry no provenance (H-01, H-02, T-03)

2. Draft · reads each AI draft beside its source and date → judges freshness without trusting the tool blindly · no source age (H-02, T-06)

3. Verify · sends only flagged answers to security, with the changed control → a decision, not a Slack question · 3 to 4 day waits, repeat questions (T-02, T-05)

4. Submit and learn · resolves every flag, saves security decisions to the library → nothing goes out "to confirm" · unchecked answers signed (H-05, T-05, T-01)

Competitive advantages

1) Flags affected answers when the change happens, not after a customer audit finds them (T-01). 2) Sends security only flagged answers, cutting repeat questions across concurrent deals (T-05). 3) Gives every answer an owner, source and date, replacing trust on faith with a check (H-02, T-03).

  • Sample size per arm use the calculator in the builder, baseline + MDE: 120 questionnaires per arm (240 in total), from the builder's calculator: baseline 58%, MDE 17 points, 80% power, 5% significance. This is the hard part for a B2B product. So the pilot pools 8 design-partner companies of the same size (hypothetical), about 25 questionnaires a week.
  • Traffic split & test duration 50/50 standard · cover ≥ 2 weekly cycles: 50 / 50 by questionnaire: each new questionnaire is randomly assigned to A or B when it arrives, behind a per-questionnaire feature flag (low volume and high stakes, so a flag gives instant rollback). 12 weeks: about 300 questionnaires expected, which reaches 240 with a buffer for withdrawn deals, and covers 12 weekly cycles and a quarter-end peak.

Define your control and variant

  • Control (A) the current experience, reference your M2 moment of misery and M3 funnel/workflow data: Today's process. The SE copies answers from the closest previous questionnaire, lets the AI drafting tool fill the gaps, and judges freshness only by the library's last-modified date. Uncertain answers go to security's Slack channel, where the median reply takes 3.5 days. At the deadline, anything unresolved is marked "to confirm" and sent. Per the M3 snapshot (hypothetical): 70% of questionnaires need security, only 30% get every answer back before the due date, 42% go out with at least one "to confirm" answer, and median turnaround is 5.2 days. Nothing tells the SE which answers a change has made outdated (the M2 moment of misery).
  • Variant (B) your single change, copy the relevant screens & functional requirements from your M4 PRD: Exactly one change: before submitting, the SE runs the Pre-Submission Answer Check (M4 PRD). Screen 1: questionnaire overview with deal name, due date, the drafted answers and a "Run check" button. Screen 2: check results with flagged answers on top; each flag shows the answer, the conflicting control, its current value and its change date, with Fix and Send to security actions. Screen 3: "Ready to submit" summary. Requirements: compares every answer with the posture register (certifications with scope, regions with status, policies with current values, each with a change date) in under 2 seconds; flags 100% of answers that contradict it; answers naming a control not in the register are marked "Unknown, send to security" and can't be cleared by the SE; sent answers show "Waiting on security"; "Ready to submit" stays disabled until every flag is resolved. Send to security posts the flagged answer, with the changed control attached, to the same security Slack channel the control uses. No new queue and no time-saved estimate.
  • Isolation check, what has NOT changed? list everything identical between arms (app version, recommendation engine, notifications, onboarding). If something changed inadvertently, your test is compromised.: Identical in both arms: the same SEs (every SE works on both A and B questionnaires), the same AI drafting tool and answer library (the check never writes back to the library), the same security reviewers, Slack channel and reply process, the same posture register content, the same buyer portals and export format, and the same deal mix and due dates (random assignment). No SE training beyond a 10-minute walkthrough of the check, given to every SE before the test starts. The only thing an SE sees differently is the check on variant questionnaires.

Module 6

Individual Insights

Where the work was hardest, the moment my thinking changed, and what I learned.

Friction points

Working on my own B2B problem meant there was no course dataset. Every builder was set up for StreamLine or RouteLogic, so I had to create hypothetical data, label it clearly, and keep asking whether a number was evidence or my assumption. The hardest moment was Module 5: one company sends about 13 security questionnaires a month, and the calculator said I needed 120 per arm. My 8-week, 20-questionnaire test from Module 3 could never have reached significance. I worked through it by pooling 8 design-partner companies over 12 weeks and making the floor (+17 points) the number to test, not the goal (+27).

Aha moment

Module 3, when the data showed that 92% of questionnaires were fully drafted within 2 days. I had assumed the problem was writing answers. It wasn't: the problem was checking them. Security review took 3.5 days against a 1-day target, and that wait is why answers go out marked "to confirm". That changed the whole project from "help write answers faster" to "check answers are still true before they're sent", and it's why I cut the AI drafting features from the roadmap.

Key learnings

1. AI tools over-value AI features. In Module 4 the AI scored "auto-complete the whole questionnaire" as a top item because it sounds fast. My hypothetical research notes and data said drafting wasn't the pain, so I overrode it. I can only critique the output because I know the domain.
2. A prototype is a test of the PRD. Building it showed rules I hadn't written: what happens to unflagged answers, how to catch a control the register has never heard of, and what happens when security asks for an edit.
3. B2B experiments are short on sample. Decide the smallest result worth shipping, fix the read date before the test starts, and don't look early.
4. The goal decides the audience. By launch, the sales engineers already knew the check from the pilot. What wasn't proven was whether anyone would pay, so the launch had to be about conversion, not awareness.
