## Friction points

_The biggest technical or strategic challenge you faced while developing your product concept._

I didn't have any course data for my problem, so I had to build my own hypothetical data and keep asking myself whether a number was evidence or just my assumption. The hardest moment was Module 5. A typical company sends about 13 security questionnaires a month, but the calculator said I needed 120 per group, so my first test plan could never have worked. I fixed it by pooling 8 design-partner companies over 12 weeks and testing the smallest result worth shipping (+17 points) instead of my goal (+27).

## Key learnings

_A few surprising discoveries or insights you gained from the course overall._

1. AI tools overrate AI features. The AI ranked "auto-complete the whole questionnaire" near the top, but my research said drafting wasn't the pain, so I overruled it. I could only do that because I know the domain.
2. Building a prototype tests the PRD. It showed me rules I hadn't written down yet.
3. B2B experiments don't get many samples. I learned to pick the smallest win worth shipping, set the read date up front, and not look early.
4. The goal decides the audience. My pilot users already knew the product, so my launch focused on getting people to pay, not on awareness.

## Aha! moment

_Your main "aha" moment during the project process._

In Module 3 the data showed that 92% of questionnaires were fully drafted within 2 days. I had assumed the problem was writing answers, but it was really checking them. Security review took 3.5 days against a 1-day target. That changed my whole project from "help write answers faster" to "check answers are still true before they're sent", and it's why I cut the AI drafting features.
