## Friction points

_The biggest technical or strategic challenge you faced while developing your product concept._

Working on my own B2B problem meant there was no course dataset. Every builder was set up for StreamLine or RouteLogic, so I had to create hypothetical data, label it clearly, and keep asking whether a number was evidence or my assumption. The hardest moment was Module 5: one company sends about 13 security questionnaires a month, and the calculator said I needed 120 per arm. My 8-week, 20-questionnaire test from Module 3 could never have reached significance. I worked through it by pooling 8 design-partner companies over 12 weeks and making the floor (+17 points) the number to test, not the goal (+27).

## Key learnings

_A few surprising discoveries or insights you gained from the course overall._

1. AI tools over-value AI features. In Module 4 the AI scored "auto-complete the whole questionnaire" as a top item because it sounds fast. My hypothetical research notes and data said drafting wasn't the pain, so I overrode it. I can only critique the output because I know the domain.
2. A prototype is a test of the PRD. Building it showed rules I hadn't written: what happens to unflagged answers, how to catch a control the register has never heard of, and what happens when security asks for an edit.
3. B2B experiments are short on sample. Decide the smallest result worth shipping, fix the read date before the test starts, and don't look early.
4. The goal decides the audience. By launch, the sales engineers already knew the check from the pilot. What wasn't proven was whether anyone would pay, so the launch had to be about conversion, not awareness.

## Aha! moment

_Your main "aha" moment during the project process._

Module 3, when the data showed that 92% of questionnaires were fully drafted within 2 days. I had assumed the problem was writing answers. It wasn't: the problem was checking them. Security review took 3.5 days against a 1-day target, and that wait is why answers go out marked "to confirm". That changed the whole project from "help write answers faster" to "check answers are still true before they're sent", and it's why I cut the AI drafting features from the roadmap.
