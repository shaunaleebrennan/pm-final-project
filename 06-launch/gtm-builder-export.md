# GTM Launch Plan, My own product

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Feature | Pre-Submission Answer Check (my M4 Now feature, tested in M5): before a security questionnaire is sent, it checks every answer against current certifications, regions and policies, flags the ones a change has made outdated, and lets the sales engineer fix them or send only those to security. |
| Goal | Conversion |
| Launch tier | M, Targeted |

## Goal & Audience
- **Goal:** Conversion, The people who matter first already know the check: 8 design-partner companies will have used it on half their questionnaires for 12 weeks. Awareness would be the wrong goal, because nobody outside a small set of mid-market SaaS companies needs to hear about it yet, and the experiment already measures whether sales engineers use it. What the bet hasn't proven is that a company will pay for it, so the launch has to turn pilot results into signed contracts.
- **Target audience:** The budget owner and the approver at the 8 hypothetical design-partner companies: the head of sales engineering (signs) and the security lead (has to trust the check). All are mid-market SaaS companies sending about 13 security questionnaires a month with no proposal team. Second, a named list of 30 lookalike companies with the same profile (hypothetical). The users stay my M2 persona, the sales engineers who run the check.

## Launch Tier
- **M, Targeted**, Reach is small: 8 companies sending about 100 questionnaires a month, plus 30 named prospects. Revenue impact is the bet's first paid contracts, which decides whether it gets funded past the pilot, but it isn't a market moment. Silence would let the design partners treat it as a free pilot that quietly ends at week 12, with no proof anyone will pay. Not L: there's no market to make aware, and paid media can't reach 38 named companies better than a direct conversation.

## Channels
1. **Owned: Pilot results readout with each design partner in week 13: a 30-minute call with their head of sales engineering and security lead, showing their own clean-questionnaire rate, turnaround and the answers the check caught, then the contract offer. They already meet us every two weeks during the pilot, so this is where they are.**
2. **Earned: Design-partner references: 2 case studies and reference calls from security leads who agree to them. Mid-market buyers check with peers before buying anything that touches security answers, and a security lead saying "I trust what my SEs send now" is more credible than anything we say.**
3. **Owned: Direct outreach from our account executive to the head of sales engineering at each of the 30 named lookalike companies, warm where possible: each design partner that signs is asked for 2 introductions to peers. The email leads with a case study and an offer to run the check on one questionnaire they've already sent. These companies aren't in our ecosystem yet, so this is the only channel that reaches them. No paid channel: an M launch doesn't justify media spend, and an ad can't target 30 named companies better than one email.**

## Enablement & Assets
An enablement brief for the account executive and customer success: what it does (checks drafted answers against current certifications, regions and policies before submission and sends only flagged answers to security), what it doesn't do (draft answers, or replace security review), what changes for SEs (one check before submitting) and for security (fewer, more specific questions), and the 3 objections we expect, with answers: "our AI answer tool already keeps the library fresh" (it's fresh when someone uploads a new document; the check is triggered by the change), "one more step for SEs" (M5 turnaround guardrail), and "who keeps the posture register current" (a named owner at the customer, set up by CS). Assets: a one-page pilot results summary, a 3-minute demo, the 2 case studies, a register setup checklist and design-partner pricing.

## Ownership, Budget & Timeline
- **Ownership & budget:** Me (PM): the go/no-go at the week-12 read, each design partner's results summary, design-partner pricing (with finance, set by week 10), the launch tracker and the metrics. Account executive (one person): readouts, contracts and the 30-company outreach. Product marketing manager (one person): enablement brief, one-pager and case studies, including asking design partners for case-study permission during Phase 1. Customer success manager (one person): register setup at each new customer, the 30-day check-in, and making sure each customer names one person who keeps their posture register current. Pilot engineer (one person): switches the check on for every questionnaire and owns the feature flag. The team is hypothetical, so these are roles; each gets a name before sign-off. Costs extra, to request before week 10: case-study writing and design (about 2 weeks of a freelancer), a year-one design-partner discount (finance sign-off before the readouts), and about a week of engineering for a demo on realistic data, because the prototype (answer-check-prototype.lovable.app) uses a hard-coded sample questionnaire. No media budget. Gap: no one on our side checks that a customer's posture register stays current after setup. The plan asks each customer to name an owner, and the customer success manager checks the register's last-changed dates at the 30-day check-in, but there's no ongoing check yet, so the check can go stale the same way the answers did.
- **Timeline:** Phase 0, validate first (2 weeks, before the pilot): run the check backwards on questionnaires that 3 to 5 companies have already sent, and count how many signed answers were already outdated, and in which categories. This replaces my hypothetical evidence with real data, tests whether a posture register can be built at all, and gives a first read on what the check is worth to a buyer for pricing. If it finds nothing, the pilot doesn't start. Phase 1, beta (weeks 1 to 12): the M5 A/B test with 8 design partners doubles as the beta. Collect objections, quotes and case-study permission; enablement brief, one-pager and demo ready by week 10. Read date: end of week 12. Phase 2, launch moment (weeks 13 to 14, only if the read says Ship): readouts and contract offers to all 8, the check switched on for every questionnaire at partners who sign, case studies published, outreach to the 30 companies starts. Phase 3, post-launch (weeks 15 to 26): conversions and usage reviewed weekly, 30-day check-ins, post-launch decision at the end of week 26.

## Success Metrics
- **Metrics:** 1) Design-partner conversion: paid contracts signed within 30 days of the readout, target 6 of 8. 2) Lookalike pipeline: of the 30 named companies, 6 run the check on a past questionnaire by week 20 and 2 sign by week 26. 3) Retention signal at paying customers: the check runs on at least 90% of questionnaires, and questionnaires with no "to confirm" answers stay at or above 75% (my M5 ship floor) for 8 weeks after launch. All targets are hypothetical.
- **Bad signal to watch for:** Design partners keep using it but won't sign: the check runs on most questionnaires, yet fewer than 4 of 8 have signed 30 days after the readout. That's trials with no upgrades, a value-proposition problem: the SE feels the value but the budget owner doesn't. The fix is the pitch (lead with security review time saved and deals that didn't slip), not a longer pilot. Early warning at a paying customer: the check runs on fewer than 70% of questionnaires by week 4 after launch, which means SEs are skipping it and the renewal is at risk; the customer success manager calls that customer's head of sales engineering that week. For the 30 lookalike companies: fewer than 3 replies by week 18 means the message isn't landing with people who haven't seen the pilot; I change the email's lead, not the list.
- **Likely post-launch decision:** Most likely Iterate: change one variable, the pitch to the budget owner, because 8 design partners is a small base and first contracts usually stall on price or on who owns the posture register. Double down if 6 of 8 sign and the 75% clean rate holds for 8 weeks: take it to the next 100 lookalike companies as an L launch. Deprioritize if fewer than 3 sign and the clean rate falls back toward the 58% baseline.
