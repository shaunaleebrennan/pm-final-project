# Strategic Discovery Map (Module 1)

## Responses
- **Write one feature from the map that, if an engineer asked “why is this a priority for our 12-week roadmap?”, you honestly could not give a strategic answer for.:** Hydration score. V1 put it under Insights with no user, no goal and no definition. I couldn't tell an engineer what it measures, who asked for it, or which metric it moves, so it would cost a sprint to build a number nobody needs. It's a feature-factory reflex: insight apps have scores.
- **Write one feature that looks “correct” for the app but has zero strategic weight, you have no idea if it helps the company make money or whether a single user actually asked for it.:** Smart bottle connections. Under Integrations it looks like an obvious, "complete" feature, but nothing says a single user asked for it or that it helps the company make money, and it pulls in hardware partners, support and a much smaller market. V3 later ranked it a money pit. (My V2 run only gave me a one-line summary, so this is taken from the feature set V1 and V2 shared.)
- **Compare V3 to your Step 1 baseline. What changed, and is the output objectively better, did the AI get smarter, or did your added PM knowledge (JTBD, effort/impact, retention) force it to be useful?:** V1 was a flat list of 18 features across six branches with no user and no ranking. V3 started from a specific user (busy adults aged 25 to 40 who forget, not who lack willpower), a job statement, and ranked every feature by effort and impact. The V1 extras fell away: themes became a fill-in, smart bottles a money pit, and hydration score didn't make the cut. V3 also named retention risks (notification fatigue, the guilt spiral after a missed day) and real tensions, like recall vs nagging and monetising the habit vs the data. The AI didn't get smarter. The JTBD, effort/impact and retention framing I added to the prompt forced it to make choices instead of listing everything.
- **Key takeaway: how does this prove the PM’s role is to think while the AI’s role is to execute?:** Prompting without context is expensive guessing.
