## Responses

- **Feature with no strategic answer (why is this a 12-week priority?):** Social leaderboards. Nothing in the map said who the user was, what the company was trying to achieve, or that anyone wanted to compete on water. It was there because "engagement apps have leaderboards", a feature-factory reflex rather than a customer need or business goal.
- **Feature that looks "correct" but has zero strategic weight:** A drink-type library with hydration percentages. It looks thorough and user-centred, but nobody asked for it, it doesn't touch the problem the map itself named (people forget to log), and it has no link to revenue or retention. It's organised, not prioritised.
- **V3 vs your Step 1 baseline — what changed, and was it your PM knowledge that forced usefulness?:** Same model, very different map. V1 was a feature list; V3 had a target user, a reason for each idea, a way to rank them, and named trade-offs (more reminders lift logging but drive uninstalls; a formed habit can mean churn). The AI didn't get smarter. The prompt carried the strategy (who it's for, JTBD, effort vs impact, retention), and the AI organised around it. The PM thinks; the AI executes.
