## Define your experiment parameters
- **Feature under test pull from your M4 PRD:** The Pre-Submission Answer Check from my M4 PRD: it compares every drafted answer with the current posture register and flags the ones a certification, region or policy change has made outdated, so the SE can fix them or send only those to security.
- **Persona pull your M2 persona:** A sales engineer at a mid-market SaaS company who answers buyer security questionnaires against deal deadlines. They can't tell which answers a certification, region or policy change has made outdated, and security takes 3 to 4 days to reply, so answers go out marked "to confirm".
- **Expected outcome the behaviour change you expect, from your M3 hypothesis:** SEs fix flagged answers or get them confirmed by security before the deadline, instead of sending them marked "to confirm".
- **Primary success metric the one number that defines success, from M3:** % of questionnaires submitted with no "to confirm" answers. Read from the file actually sent to the buyer, not the tool's status: a questionnaire is clean if no answer is marked "to confirm", "TBC" or left blank waiting on security.
- **Baseline rate today's rate of your primary metric, from your M3 data:** 58% of questionnaires go out with no "to confirm" answers today (42% have at least one). Hypothetical M3 snapshot: 40 questionnaires in 90 days at one mid-market SaaS company.
- **Guardrail metric & boundary what must not break, and how far it can move before you investigate:** Guardrail: median questionnaire turnaround (received to submitted), 5.2 days today. Boundary: the variant arm may not run more than 0.5 day slower than the control arm (about 5.7 days); cross it and I investigate before any rollout. Second guardrail, outdated answers that still get sent: a security reviewer spot-checks 10 random high-risk answers per submitted questionnaire, blind to arm. The variant's outdated rate must not be higher than control's.
- **Minimum Detectable Effect (MDE) the smallest improvement worth shipping, your floor:** +17 points (58% to 75%). That's the "scale" threshold I set in M3: below 75% the check isn't worth rolling out, even if it helps. 85% stays the goal, not the floor.
- **Sample size per arm use the calculator in the builder, baseline + MDE:** 120 questionnaires per arm (240 in total), from the builder's calculator: baseline 58%, MDE 17 points, 80% power, 5% significance. This is the hard part for a B2B product. One company sends about 13 questionnaires a month, so M3's 8 weeks and 20 questionnaires would give about 10 per arm, which couldn't detect even a jump to 99%. So the pilot pools 8 design-partner companies of the same size (hypothetical), about 25 questionnaires a week.
- **Traffic split & test duration 50/50 standard · cover ≥ 2 weekly cycles:** 50 / 50 by questionnaire: each new questionnaire is randomly assigned to A or B when it arrives, behind a per-questionnaire feature flag (low volume and high stakes, so a flag gives instant rollback). 12 weeks: about 300 questionnaires expected, which reaches 240 with a buffer for withdrawn deals, and covers 12 weekly cycles and a quarter-end peak.
- **Significance threshold p < 0.05 is standard, explain any deviation:** p < 0.05 (95%), two-sided, no deviation. I considered p < 0.10 (95 per arm) to shorten the test, but a false Ship means SEs trust a check that misses outdated answers, so I kept the standard.

## Define your control and variant
- **Control (A) the current experience, reference your M2 moment of misery and M3 funnel/workflow data:** Today's process. The SE copies answers from the closest previous questionnaire, lets the AI drafting tool fill the gaps, and judges freshness only by the library's last-modified date. Uncertain answers go to security's Slack channel, where the median reply takes 3.5 days. At the deadline, anything unresolved is marked "to confirm" and sent. Per the M3 snapshot (hypothetical): 70% of questionnaires need security, only 30% get every answer back before the due date, 42% go out with at least one "to confirm" answer, and median turnaround is 5.2 days. Nothing tells the SE which answers a change has made outdated (the M2 moment of misery).
- **Variant (B) your single change, copy the relevant screens & functional requirements from your M4 PRD:** Exactly one change: before submitting, the SE runs the Pre-Submission Answer Check (M4 PRD). Screen 1: questionnaire overview with deal name, due date, the drafted answers and a "Run check" button. Screen 2: check results with flagged answers on top; each flag shows the answer, the conflicting control, its current value and its change date, with Fix and Send to security actions. Screen 3: "Ready to submit" summary. Requirements: compares every answer with the posture register (certifications with scope, regions with status, policies with current values, each with a change date) in under 2 seconds; flags 100% of answers that contradict it; answers naming a control not in the register are marked "Unknown, send to security" and can't be cleared by the SE; sent answers show "Waiting on security"; "Ready to submit" stays disabled until every flag is resolved. Send to security posts the flagged answer, with the changed control attached, to the same security Slack channel the control uses. No new queue and no time-saved estimate.
- **Isolation check, what has NOT changed? list everything identical between arms (app version, recommendation engine, notifications, onboarding). If something changed inadvertently, your test is compromised.:** Identical in both arms: the same SEs (every SE works on both A and B questionnaires), the same AI drafting tool and answer library (the check never writes back to the library), the same security reviewers, Slack channel and reply process, the same posture register content, the same buyer portals and export format, and the same deal mix and due dates (random assignment). No SE training beyond a 10-minute walkthrough of the check, given to every SE before the test starts. The only thing an SE sees differently is the check on variant questionnaires.

## Formalize your hypothesis & shipping criteria
- **Your hypothesis (filled in):** I believe that the Pre-Submission Answer Check (flags answers a certification, region or policy change has made outdated) for mid-market sales engineers who answer buyer security questionnaires against deal deadlines will result in sales engineers fixing flagged answers or getting them confirmed by security before the deadline, instead of sending them marked "to confirm", as measured by a +17 pts (58% to 75%) change in % of questionnaires submitted with no "to confirm" answers within 12 weeks. We will protect median questionnaire turnaround (5.2 days today) throughout the test.
- **Your shipping criteria (filled in):** We will SHIP if % of questionnaires submitted with no "to confirm" answers improves by ≥ 17 points (58% to 75% or better) at p < 0.05, median turnaround in the variant is no more than 0.5 day slower than control, and the blind spot-check finds no more outdated answers in the variant than in control, after 12 weeks and at least 240 questionnaires. We will ITERATE if direction is positive but the lift is below 17 points: sharpen the flags or the register and re-run. We will INVESTIGATE if the primary metric improves but a guardrail breaks, or if companies or SE segments (mid-market vs. new-hire) move in opposite directions. We will KILL if the primary metric shows no improvement or moves negatively. The read date is fixed at the end of week 12, with no results reviewed before then; if fewer than 240 questionnaires have come in by then, the result can't trigger a Ship, only Iterate or Kill.
- **Hardest parameter to define, and did it change your hypothesis? quick debrief:** Sample size and duration. The calculator said 120 questionnaires per arm, and one company sends about 13 a month, so my M3 window (8 weeks, 20 questionnaires) could never reach significance. That changed my hypothesis: the number is now the +17-point floor, not the +27 target, the window is 12 weeks and 240 questionnaires across 8 design-partner companies, and the read date is fixed. Running the pressure-test prompt also changed three things: I took the new security queue and the time-saved banner out of the variant so only the check differs, I turned "must not increase" into a +0.5 day boundary against control, and I added a blind spot-check guardrail because the submit lock could push SEs to clear answers without checking them.
